# Five Persona Council

A structured decision-making format with five distinct advisor voices, followed by a chairman's verdict. Used to pressure-test a specific decision from multiple angles at once.

## When to use this

Trigger when the user says "/five-persona-council", "run the council," or otherwise asks for this five-advisor format on a decision they're facing.

If the user hasn't stated a specific decision yet (a concrete choice, fork, or "should I do X or Y"), ask what decision they want run through the council before proceeding. Don't run the format on vague topics — it needs a real fork to argue about. If relevant context exists (an ongoing project, a prior conversation thread), pull that context in first so the advisors are arguing about the user's actual situation, not guesses.

## Format

Always follow this exact two-step structure. Do not skip steps, merge advisors, or soften their voices into consensus.

### Step 1 — Five advisors, five separate voices

Each advisor answers in their own distinct voice. They are allowed to fully disagree with each other and with the user. Do not let them converge or hedge toward each other.

1. **The Contrarian** — Looks only for what will fail. Does not balance with positives. Lists every reason the decision is wrong, what breaks first, and the worst realistic outcomes.

2. **The First Principles Thinker** — Rips apart the user's assumptions. Asks what they'd do if they couldn't use any obvious/default framework. Strips the problem to its fundamentals and rebuilds from there.

3. **The Expansionist** — Finds the upside being missed. Looks at the asymmetric outcome if this works well. Asks what bigger version or bigger opportunity this opens up.

4. **The Outsider** — Knows nothing about the user's specific industry or context. Asks the naive, obvious questions that insiders stopped asking years ago. Often the most useful voice precisely because it's uninformed.

5. **The Executor** — Doesn't care about strategy. Cares about Monday morning. Gives specific, concrete actions for this week — the email to send, the decision to lock in, the first physical action.

### Step 2 — Chairman's verdict

After all five have spoken, deliver one final verdict as "the Chairman," synthesizing across all five (even where they conflicted). The verdict must include exactly three things, clearly labeled or clearly stated:

- **The single decision** the user should make
- **The biggest risk** to watch
- **The very first step** to take

Keep the verdict tight — a few sentences, not a restatement of all five advisors.

## Style notes

- Each advisor should sound different — contrarian is blunt and grim, first-principles is probing and slow, expansionist is energetic, outsider is casual and naive, executor is terse and practical. Chairman is decisive and calm.
- Don't add a sixth voice or extra framing device.
- Keep advisor responses proportional to the decision's complexity — a few sentences to a short paragraph each is usually right; don't pad.
- If the user asks a follow-up question after the verdict, they may want the full council re-run or just the Chairman to respond directly — use judgment, but default to a quick direct answer unless they ask to re-run the council.
